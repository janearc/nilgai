# firang: the first pass

2026-11-06, unreviewed, from a scratch program.

29,947,046 stars, kept at a parallax of at least five times its error,
ran back ten million years at both estimates of the Sun's speed along
the rotation. 57,532 passed within a kiloparsec of Sgr A*. Each of
those ran back 100 more times across its errors.

To test the selection, 20,000 fake stars were thrown from Sgr A* and
observed with real Gaia errors. Traced back from their true values,
nine in ten land within half a parsec of it.

Of the fake stars seen within 3 kiloparsecs of us with good
parallaxes, three in four have at least a fifth of their draws pass
within 100 parsecs.

No star is shown to have come from Sgr A*. Five fast stars, with
radial velocities well measured, are consistent with it:

```
4037334576111139712   467 km/s   half its draws within 100 pc
4385671099714045824   487 km/s   35%
6736906690592556672   401 km/s   31%
4081136684118965760   405 km/s   22%
6760416924931944832   402 km/s   22%
```

Sixteen faster stars close to the Sun looked like thrown stars. They
miss Sgr A* by hundreds of parsecs, where thrown stars seen as well
would not.

They belong to a population of huge radial velocities in both signs
across the sky, in faint stars near us: most likely measurement
trouble. That reading is under independent review: [sixteen.md](sixteen.md).

The tool, cmd/firang, run the same morning, found the same 57,532 in
the screen and the same control: 111 of 151 thrown stars seen near us
with a fifth of their draws within 100 parsecs. Of the five, four met
the numbers again; 6760416924931944832 drew 17%, under the cut.

A hundred draws hold a share to about four points, so a star at the
cut can fall either side from one run to the next. More draws would
settle it; that number is for the search to choose.

spectra.
